# Your Plateau Citizen ID

Plain-language guidance for residents of Plateau State. This describes what the
platform does with your information and what you can do about it.

## What a PCID is

A Plateau Citizen ID is the state's own way of referring to you across government
services. It looks like this:

```
PL-4K7T9-QM2XB-7H
```

It is generated at random. It contains **nothing about you** — not your date of
birth, not your Local Government Area, not your sex, not your name, not your phone
number, not your National Identification Number. Nobody can work anything out from
it, and nobody can guess somebody else's.

Once issued, it is yours for life and is never reused for anyone else, even if
your record is later closed.

## You do not need a NIN

A PCID does not depend on the national identity number. You can hold a PCID
without a NIN. If a government agency has recorded your NIN, it is kept as an
extra reference and is treated as some of the most restricted information in the
system.

## Getting one

At a registration desk you provide your name, date of birth, sex, and how to
contact you. If your details closely match someone already registered, the
registration stops and a person reviews it before anything is issued — the system
never merges two people's records on its own.

You will also be given credentials for the citizen portal. These are issued in
person, deliberately: an open sign-up page would let anyone who knows a PCID claim
somebody's record.

## What you can do

**See your own record**, including the government records linked to it — property,
vehicles, business registrations, licences, revenue.

**Manage your emergency contacts.** These are the people called if you are in an
accident. They are yours to manage. If a government officer changes one, the
record shows that they did.

**Choose what emergency responders can see.** You can record your blood group and
any critical conditions or allergies. This is released **only** to emergency
responders attending an actual incident, and never for any other purpose — not
for a tax enquiry, not for a licence application, not to a ministry.

**See who has looked at your record**: which agency, when, and for what purpose.

**Ask for a correction** if something is wrong, with your reasons and any
evidence. It goes to the agency that owns the record.

**Report an emergency**, and **report someone missing**.

## What an emergency responder sees

If you are found unconscious after an accident and a responder scans your card,
they see:

- your name;
- your approximate age — not your date of birth;
- your sex;
- your photograph, to confirm you are who the card says;
- your Local Government Area;
- your emergency contacts;
- your blood group and any conditions you chose to record.

They do not see your address, your phone number, your NIN, your tax records, your
property, or anything about any case. They see what is needed to treat you and to
reach your family.

Their access is tied to that specific emergency and ends when it does.

## What government agencies see

Not everything, and not because of who they are.

Every officer who looks at your record must say **why** — for a service, to verify
your identity, for an emergency, for an investigation. The system checks that the
reason is one their job allows, and then releases only the specific pieces of
information that reason justifies.

A revenue officer dealing with a tax matter cannot see your medical information —
not because they have been asked not to, but because the system will not release
it for that reason.

An investigator cannot look you up on a hunch. They must have an open case, be
assigned to it, and record in writing why you are relevant to it before your
record opens.

Every single access is recorded permanently, and the record cannot be altered or
deleted by anyone.

## Your access history

The portal shows who looked at your record, when and why.

One exception: if you are the subject of an active criminal investigation, that
access is withheld from this list — otherwise telling you would defeat the
investigation. When that happens:

- the list tells you that some access may be withheld, rather than pretending to
  be complete;
- a legal reason is recorded for it;
- the state's Data Protection Officer can see what you cannot, and can review
  whether withholding it was justified.

## What the system will never do

- **Track where you are.** It holds your registered address — where government
  records say you live — and nothing that observes where you actually are. There
  is no ability to locate you, and none can be switched on without a new law, a
  new module, and a new assessment.
- **Score you, or predict anything about you.** Nothing in it rates people as
  risky or likely to do anything. No decision anywhere uses your ethnicity,
  religion, politics, or how wealthy your area is.
- **Decide anything about you automatically.** Where the system suggests that two
  records might be the same person, a person reviews it and decides.
- **Let anyone download the register.** There is no facility to export it. Nobody
  has it, including the people who run the platform.
- **Store your fingerprints or face.** It holds neither and matches neither.

## Protecting your account

- Use a long passphrase you have not used anywhere else.
- Keep your emergency contacts current — they matter most at the worst moment.
- Report a lost card so it can be replaced.
- If you see an access in your history you cannot account for, report it. It will
  be investigated, and the record of it cannot be erased.

## Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your information, ask for it to be corrected, and
complain about how it has been used, to the Data Protection Officer of the
Government of Plateau State.

Your identity record itself cannot be deleted — it is a statutory government
register, like a birth record — but you can ask for it to be corrected and you can
see who has used it.

## If something is wrong

Go to any registration desk, or use the portal. Quote the reference shown with any
error message: it lets support find exactly what happened without either of you
having to exchange personal details over the phone.
